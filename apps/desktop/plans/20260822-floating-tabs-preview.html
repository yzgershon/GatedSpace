Floating tabs, and taking tabs out of the workspace

Two changes that only make sense together. The look: one expanded tab, the rest collapsed to a mark, opening on hover. The architecture: a tab stops being a child of whichever workspace the sidebar has selected and becomes a thing with its own identity that carries its workspace with it.

Everything below is at 1:1 and uses the app's real tokens. Nothing here is built. Hover the collapsed tabs — the expansion is live, not a picture of one.

The rail sits in the gap you named: right of the agent presets, left of the shells chip and Open in. It is anchored to the RIGHT and grows leftward, so expanding a tab moves nothing on the right-hand side. The collapsed marks still report status through the ring — amber pulsing is working, green is done and unseen, red is an error.

Why the ring and not a separate dot. A collapsed tab is about 17px wide. A mark plus a dot inside that is two objects fighting for the same few pixels, and the dot is the one that loses. Wrapping the mark in the colour makes the status the size of the whole tab, which is what you actually want to catch from across the room. Variant B keeps them separate if you would rather.

Behaviour, and the things that must not break

  • Hover opens on a delay, closes on a longer one. Roughly 120ms in, 260ms out. Without it the rail flickers every time the pointer crosses it on the way to Open in.
  • The rail is a drop target. Dragging a pane onto empty rail space gives it its own group. That is the ONLY one-gesture way to do it, and it is the thing that nearly died with the tab strip — the group pill exists today largely to keep it alive.
  • Drag to reorder stays. Tabs reorder within the rail as they do in the strip.
  • Double-click the active tab renames the group. It has no other home once the pill is gone.
  • Expansion animates max-width, not width. A hardcoded expanded width truncates the one name you hovered in order to read.
The failure mode to design against. Collapsed tabs are only worth it if you can still tell WHICH is which without hovering all of them. Four tabs all running Claude collapse to four identical orange marks. So the mark is the AGENT, the ring is the STATUS, and the tab keeps its pane count on expand. Where several tabs share an agent, the sidebar is the fallback — it already lists them as text rows with their names.

Taking tabs out of the workspace

This is the part that is real work rather than styling. Today a tab cannot exist outside a workspace, and the code is explicit about why.

How it works now

  • WorkspaceState { version, tabs[], activeTabId } is persisted into v2WorkspaceLocalState.paneLayout, keyed by workspaceId.
  • useV2WorkspacePaneLayout builds a new store per workspaceId (useMemo([workspaceId])). Its comment says why: during fast workspace switches live queries briefly return stale rows, and one shared store let panes from one worktree render and persist under another.
  • So switching workspace swaps the entire tab set. The strip is a view of the sidebar's selection, not a view of your work.

What it becomes

  • One tab list for the app. Each tab records the workspaceId its panes belong to.
  • Clicking a tab switches the workspace to that tab's workspace, rather than switching workspace replacing the tabs. The rail shows all your work; the sidebar becomes how you START something rather than what you are limited to.
  • Persistence moves from one blob per workspace to one record per tab, so two workspaces can no longer write over each other's layout.
What "separate from workspace" cannot mean, and this is a real limit. A tab still has to name ONE workspace, because its panes do. host-service refuses a terminal whose workspace does not match the one that owns it (getTerminalWorkspaceMismatchError), and a session pane's cwd comes from the workspace's worktree path. A tab holding panes from three workspaces would be three different answers to "what does this terminal belong to". So: tabs stop being FILTERED by the selected workspace, and each tab still carries its own.
The isolation guarantee has to survive the move. The per-workspace store exists to stop cross-worktree bleed. A single global store gives that up unless panes validate their tab's workspace before mounting and persistence writes per tab. That check is not optional — it is the whole reason the current design looks the way it does, and skipping it reintroduces a bug that was already paid for once.

Order I would build it

  1. The rail, still per-workspace. Pure UI: new component, new top-bar slot, pill deleted. Nothing about persistence moves. This is the half you can look at and judge, and it is reversible.
  2. The migration. Flatten every paneLayout into one list stamped with its workspaceId, behind a version bump, with the old blob left in place until it is confirmed.
  3. Cross-workspace switching. Clicking a tab navigates. This is the one that can break session ownership, so it goes last and on its own.
